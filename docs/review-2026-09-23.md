# Go/no-go review — 2026-09-23

## Decision

**No-go on the tested formulation.** Across two completed benchmark families
and three frontier models, pattern-language guidance produced no measurable
advantage over a direct procedure or a checklist — and on the differentiating
task it was the weakest arm. The mechanism analysis explains *why*, not just
that it failed: the linked procedure encodes an objective that does not match
what makes the reference partition good, and its core computation is
unexecutable in-context anyway.

Keep the evidence machinery — the frozen-plan/admission/provenance discipline,
the bounded-spend provider route, and the independent scorer are durable and
reusable. Retire the claim that prompt-time linked-procedure guidance improves
model-driven decomposition or design selection. That specific hypothesis has
now been tested to a null twice, with a mechanism-level explanation.

This is a bounded verdict: it covers the two instruments actually built
(enumerable construction and link-table decomposition), the three models run
(sonnet-4.6, opus-4.8, gpt-5.2), and the procedure formulations tested. It
does not prove no formulation of "patterns" could ever help — only that the
ones built and measured here did not, and for identifiable reasons rather
than noise.

## The completed comparison

Two independent sonnet-4.6 runs give n=24 scored calls per arm:

| Arm | valid / 24 | valid-ARI | denom-ARI | links-inside (of 144 sample) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| direct | 19 | 0.104 | 0.082 | 24.3 |
| checklist | 20 | 0.108 | 0.090 | 24.6 |
| pattern | 15 | 0.107 | 0.067 | 25.9 |

- Among **valid** artifacts the arms are statistically identical
  (~0.105 ± 0.015). The linked procedure did not recover more of the
  reference structure.
- The pattern arm produced the **fewest valid artifacts** (15/24 vs 19–20/24).
  The harder narration increases exact-coverage contract failures — that is
  the entire denominator-level deficit.
- The pattern arm did keep more sample links inside (25.9 vs ~24.4) — a weak
  trend (Welch t ≈ 1.6, not significant), and it did not translate into
  reference agreement.

opus-4.8 and gpt-5.2 fail the mechanical contract almost entirely (hallucinated
misfit ids like `m20A`, dropped members, and code injected into the JSON — one
artifact literally contains `"m46".replace("46","47")`). Sonnet-4.6 is the only
model tested that reliably enumerates the 141-item universe — a model property,
not an arm effect. Frontier capability does not rescue the contract.

## Why the linked procedure cannot win here

1. **It encodes the wrong objective.** The procedure's core step — "place each
   misfit in the group holding the most of its links" — optimizes link
   *density*. A pure density maximizer produces a degenerate partition (one
   130-misfit blob + 11 singletons holding 1338/1434 links inside). Alexander's
   reference keeps only 437/1434 (30%) inside with balanced groups (sizes
   7–23): it optimizes *functional coherence*, not density. The pattern arm's
   extra links-inside is drift *toward the degenerate criterion* — away from
   the reference, not toward it.

2. **The mechanism is unexecutable in-context.** Placing 141 misfits by
   link-majority requires ~10⁴ pairwise lookups; the model narrates the
   procedure but computes a topical approximation. All arms converge to
   ~0.10 ARI because functional/topical clustering is what the prompt's
   evidence supports — and also what the reference is. There is no headroom
   for the procedure to exploit (even pure link algorithms cap at ~0.17–0.23
   ARI and produce illegal or degenerate shapes).

The deeper observation: the procedure adds nothing because the model already
does the relevant thing by default. Semantic grouping by functional coherence
*is* the native move; the linked-construction ceremony adds narration cost
(more invalid artifacts) without adding information.

## What this does and does not settle

Settled:
- On an enumerable construction space the guidance was inert (ceiling).
- On a link-table decomposition the linked procedure did not help and was
  less reliable — reproduced across two runs, and the bigger models failed the
  task outright.
- The specific procedure tested encodes a mis-specified objective (density)
  and an unexecutable computation (in-context graph clustering).

Not settled (and not claimed):
- Whether a *different* formulation — patterns as context-selection, as a
  checklist, or embedded as tools — could help. That would be a different
  hypothesis requiring a different instrument.
- Whether the village task's reference is even recoverable in-context: it is
  Alexander's HIDECS output, and no arm (or pure algorithm) approximates it
  closely. The task may partially test memorization rather than decomposition.
